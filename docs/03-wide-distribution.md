# Wide distribution: getting into bookstores

KDP's expanded distribution is the option you already have. IngramSpark is the
compressed version. This chapter compares both, then looks at what the
*third* doors (Lulu, Draft2Digital for print, aggregators) actually change.

## First, the shape of the problem

There are three print markets in the US, roughly speaking:

1. **Amazon** — where most indies sell, and where KDP is the only sensible
   printer (fastest delivery, best royalty).
2. **The "wide" trade** — bookstores, gift shops, comic shops, and libraries
   ordering through **Ingram** (a huge wholesale network connected to almost
   every store), plus Barnes & Noble online, Books-A-Million, and regional
   wholesalers.
3. **Direct** — selling print yourself in person (chapter on direct sales).

KDP expanded distribution tries to reach market 2 *from inside Amazon*.
IngramSpark is market 2's native printer. The trouble is that both paths use
**wholesale discounts**, which are the part of the math that decides whether
a bookstore has any reason to stock your book.

## The wholesale discount, in one breath

A bookstore buys at wholesale — the store keeps a cut of your cover price as
its markup, because the store has rent. Traditional publishing sells at
**55% off** cover ("wholesale discount 55%"). As an indie you can often
choose **40% off** — but at 40%, some outlets treat your book as a special
order instead of a "stock it and see" item.

Math that decides everything: a $15.99 paperback at 55% wholesale leaves you
$7.20 before print costs. At 40% you keep $9.59. But money is not the whole
problem — the bigger one is whether the store *orders the book in the first
place*, which is why the [chapter on libraries](05-into-libraries.md)
exists.

## KDP expanded vs. IngramSpark, head to head

| | KDP expanded distribution | IngramSpark |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Free, a checkbox | Paperback about $49, hardcover about $69 (promo codes change; check current) |
| Royalty rate | **40%** of list price, after printing costs deducted | List price × wholesale discount − printing cost |
| Where it plugs in | Anywhere Ingram is (bookstores, libraries, some countries) | Same Ingram network — plus more options the other direction |
| Time to appear | Slow: weeks, and bookstores often filter it | Faster and more predictable once you price-match |
| Price-match conflicts | **KDP paperback auto-shown to rivals *and* to Ingram** if you raise prices — settings to opt out | IngramSpark auto-reconciles its price to KDP list price if you set it during setup |
| Amazon needs | Not applicable — you use KDP natively on Amazon | Can also list on Amazon, but you should **not duplicate a KDP ISBN on Amazon**; one ISBN, one store of record |
| Returns | Non-returnable | Your choice: returnable (stores like it) or non-returnable; returnable means destroyed-copies billed to you |
| Special formats | Never | Hardcovers (and real dust jackets) are a strong reason to choose IngramSpark |

> **Warning on the big gotcha:** never put the *same ISBN* on Amazon
> (via KDP) *and* on IngramSpark with significantly different list prices.
> One ISBN = one price in the eyes of the trade. Keep the list price
> identical, or use different ISBN-free "Amazon edition" vs
> "bookstore edition" numbers (see the ISBN chapter).

## The decision, without agonizing

Run your numbers in one minute each way:

1. Calculate your KDP-only list price. (Companion tool is
   [kdp-profit-planner](https://github.com/dylanasdale): royalty, break-even,
   price scans.)
2. Calculate IngramSpark at **40%** wholesale on the same list price. Note the
   printing cost and see if that royalty still feels fair.
3. Ask one question: **do I have a realistic plan for a physical
   audience?** Signings, local shops, cons, library events, a launch at a
   store that would actually take your niche.

| Your honest answer about bookstores | Do this |
|---|---|
| "Not really — my readers buy online" | KDP alone. Set expanded distribution on its checkbox and move on. The cost is zero. |
| "Yes — I have an event, a niche store, or a library contact" | Add IngramSpark on the *bookstore ISBN*; keep Amazon KDP on Amazon. Typically you add a second ISBN or reuse the Bowker one. |
| "I want a hardcover" | IngramSpark is the shortcut; KDP hardcovers exist but the dust-jacket/lithocase options at IngramSpark are the trade standard. |
| "I want refunds-proof stock runs" | IngramSpark non-returnable, and print 1–4 copies at a time with a bookshop discount — no inventory. |

## Aggregators and the print third door

For **ebooks**, the "wide" story belongs to aggregators — a related chapter of
the same map:

- **Draft2Digital** (free to use, take roughly a 10% cut from list price) can
  push your ebook to Apple Books, Kobo, Nook, Tolino, and — importantly for
  the [library chapter](05-into-libraries.md) — library platforms. For most
  indie authors, D2D plus Amazon covers the ebook map.
- **Lulu** is a strong second printer for **special formats and direct-from-
  publisher links**, but it is not the Ingram-wide phonebook that bookstore
  buyers use.
- **Bookshop.org** and indie-store-wide sites typically come *through*
  Ingram listings, not separate deals.

**Skipping to the summary table** (the round planning numbers; check current
rates on each platform's pricing page):

| Channel | Upfront | Your cut of $15.99 paperback (after print, round) | Reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| KDP Amazon only | $0 | ~ $5.60–$6.20 at 60% royalty | Amazon |
| KDP expanded on | $0 | ~ $0.90–$1.20 at 40% royalty | Amazon + Ingram late |
| IngramSpark | ~ $49 | ~ $5.70–$7.20 at 55%/40% discount math | Ingram stores and libraries *now* |
| Direct sales (ch. 6) | Setup, ~ $0–30 | ~ $12–14 | Only people you can reach yourself |

> Notice the quiet pattern: most of these "wide" paths pay you **less per
> unit** than Amazon, and only two of them get you *seen in a room where
> strangers browse* — direct sales (your own room) and library copies
> (many rooms, no marketing).
