# Putting it together: the launch sequence

Everything before this was one door at a time. Here is the whole build order,
with a budget table and the decision worksheets that keep you honest. The
assumed book: a $9.99 ebook, $16.99 paperback, ~60,000 words, produced with
the companion projects ([interior templates](https://github.com/dylanasdale),
[profit planner](https://github.com/dylanasdale)).

skip IngramSpark) is $0 and still publishes everywhere that matters most.

## The three branching decisions, honestly made

### 1. Do I buy ISBNs? *(only real cash commitment)*

Run the [channel worksheet](worksheets/worksheet-channel-decision.md). If the
answer to "will this book touch IngramSpark, bookstores, or libraries within
a year?" is yes, or *maybe* — buy the 10-pack. If the book is a
platform-exclusive experiment — skip it. You can always re-edition later;
you just reset review momentum, which the worksheet costs out.

### 2. Which audio door? *(the rights-heavy one)*

Run the [rights map worksheet](worksheets/worksheet-rights-map.md) *before*
ACX, because ACX's 7-year term is the only decision in this guide where
"change my mind later" costs a lawyer. The beginner default (non-exclusive
royalty share) survives being wrong.

### 3. Direct sales now or later? *(the audience-heavy one)*

Direct sales convert only an audience you built. Under ~300 backers of any
kind, *build the Payhip page anyway* (it costs an afternoon) and treat it as
your receipt book: every in-person copy, every newsletter launch, every
bundle lives there. The channel's real activation is whenever your list is
big enough — which is not a money decision at all.

## Two worked mini-scenarios

**Scenario A — "Amazon-first, zero-risk":** KDP paperback + ebook, expanded
distribution on, free ISBN, no audio. Cost: **$0**. Year-one income at
30 copies/month (a realistic niche title): roughly **$2,100** royalties.
Taxes on ~ $2,100 profit: roughly **$530**. That's the baseline every other
scenario has to beat.

**Scenario B — "wide launch":** same, plus Bowker 10-pack, IngramSpark at
40% discount, Payhip, ACX non-exclusive with a royalty-share narrator, D2D
wide + libraries. Cost: **about $350**. Year-one income: Amazon roughly the
same **$2,100**, plus ~ **$260** wide/library/*direct* margin (bookstores
buy small without an audience; direct is list-dependent), plus audio
(bit-early; break-even not reached in year one). Taxes on ~ $1,700 profit
after deducting the $350: roughly **$430**.

Round conclusions: **Scenario B beats A in year two or three** (if the
narrator relationship holds and libraries renew) and is close enough at
50/50 in year one that the deciding factor is *whether bookstores or
libraries fit your actual book*. The worksheets exist precisely because the
right answer depends on your book, not on a blog's enthusiasm.

## The one-page checklist to run before any "go wide" button

- [ ] Price identical across ISBN-holders (KDP list = IngramSpark list)
- [ ] Copyright page shows the right ISBN for *that* edition
- [ ] Returns decision made and recorded (IngramSpark is returnable or not)
- [ ] Wholesale discount chosen (40% vs 55%) and it agrees with the math
- [ ] BISAC subjects picked deliberately (2–3, not 12)
- [ ] Cover hardcover vs paperback files are separate and correct
- [ ] Newsletter hook inside the book points at a page that works
- [ ] Every expense this month lands in the receipt folder
- [ ] Companion [kdp-profit-planner](https://github.com/dylanasdale) says the
      price still breaks even after all of today's decisions
