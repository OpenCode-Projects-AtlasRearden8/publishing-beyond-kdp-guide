# Copyright and ISBNs demystified

Two things every book has: a copyright line, and a barcode number. Both are
mostly housekeeping — and both are surrounded by unnecessary mystery. Here is
the plain version, with the round numbers attached.

## Copyright: automatic, then strengthened

### You already own it

The instant you finish a draft to paper or file, copyright attaches in most
countries (the US included, now a member of
[Berne](https://www.copyright.gov/international-articles.html)). No form, no
fee, no "©" symbol required. If you wrote it in 2026, you own it in 2026.

So what does registration *do*? In the US, registering with the Copyright
Office:

| Step | Never registered | Registered (timely¹) |
|---|---|---|
| Prove ownership | Yes, but harder | Registration certificate |
| Statutory damages | Not available — you must show actual damages | Up to $150,000 per work (per finding of willful infringement, returned by a court) |
| Attorney fees | Almost always your own | Court may award them |
| Filing a lawsuit | Requires registration | Simplest path |

¹ *Registered before infringement, or within 3 months of publication.*

Round numbers: an online registration through
[copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov) costs about **$45–65** (single
author, single work; group and expedited registrations cost more). It
takes weeks-to-months to process. It is entirely optional for publishing.

> **Note on the mysterious "poor man's copyright"** — mailing your manuscript
> to yourself and never opening it. It proves nothing a timestamped draft
> folder doesn't, only cost in some countries, and does zero in the US. Do it
> for fun if you like stamps. Registering is the real thing.

### What the copyright page says

The copyright page (the one before chapter one) can be this small:

```text
The Massive AI Manual
Copyright (c) 2026 Asdale Dynamics, LLC
All rights reserved.
First edition: 2026
ISBN 979-8-XXXXXXX-X (paperback)          <- from the ISBN chapter
ISBN 979-8-XXXXXXX-X (ebook)
```

"Published by" lines, disclaimers ("this book is not medical advice"),
editorial credits, and edition numbers all live here and never have to go to
a lawyer. Nothing below is legally required; the © line and rights-reserved
line are worth having as a courtesy and for licensing requests.

**Wait — your book is about real companies?** If your book discusses real people or
real companies, add a plain-English disclaimer: *"All trademarks are the
property of their respective owners. This book is educational and not
affiliated with Amazon KDP or IngramSpark."* That sentence does more legal
work than the whole chapter you are reading.

## ISBNs: a barcode from one store to another

An **ISBN** (International Standard Book Number) is a 13-digit number that
lets every bookstore, library, and warehouse look up *one exact edition* of a
book. Three rules carry all the weight:

1. **One edition, one ISBN.** Paperback, hardcover, ebook, audiobook: each
   format gets its own number. If the same print edition switches printers or
   prices, the ISBN stays.
2. **The ISBN's *issuer* is recorded.** The publisher ID inside an ISBN is
   eternal metadata: "this book was published by the company that bought this
   block of ISBNs."
3. **Changing the ISBN changes the edition.** Republishing with a new ISBN
   resets sales history, reviews (often), and awards eligibility. Do it when
   the title truly changes; don't, when you just want a new cover.

### Where indie authors get ISBNs

In the US, the official registry is Bowker's
[MyIdentifiers](https://www.myidentifiers.com).

| Source | Cost (round numbers, US) | Publisher ID inside | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| KDP free ISBN | $0 | "Independently published" | Purely Amazon-first publishing |
| Bowker, single | about $125 | *Your* imprint name | Anywhere else your book appears |
| Bowker, 10 | about $295 (about $30 each) | *Your* imprint name | One title, paperback + hardcover + ebook |
| Bowker, 100 | about $575 (about $6 each) | *Your* imprint name | A career of book publishing, easily amortized |

(Prices move; treat these as planning numbers, not quotes.)

### The KDP-free-ISBN decision, without drama

KDP's free ISBN is fine *if* the book lives its whole life on Amazon. The
moment the paperback reaches IngramSpark, bookstores, or libraries, the
"Independently published" imprint follows it — some indie-boutique buyers and
review outlets notice. Also, a KDP free ISBN cannot be used for an
edition printed outside KDP.

Rule of thumb you can defend at a whiteboard:

> **Amazon-only experiment? Free ISBN. Anything else — even "maybe one day I
> will try IngramSpark? Bowker, 10-pack.** At $30 per ISBN it costs less than
> most cover designers' coffee budget, and it is the one part of publishing
> that never expires or argues.

Two ISBN footnotes that save real money:

- **Do not buy country-specific ISBNs from eBay** or any reseller. A US
  author selling only through Bowker is the cheap, boring, workable answer.
- **In the UK it's Nielsen ISBN Store, in Canada (rare case) free via
  Library and Archives Canada.** If you publish from another country, check
  its national agency before paying Bowker.

## Do I need a "Publisher" name at all?

If you will get your own ISBNs, you need an imprint name to print next to
them — that publisher line shows up in every bookstore database. Many indie
authors use the family LLC ("Asdale Dynamics Press"), or their own name.
Your business books won't care which one you choose. Pick a name you can say
out loud without cringing; *you will say it a hundred times*.
