# Rights you keep

The scariest word in self-publishing is "exclusive." The most reassuring one is
"non-exclusive." This chapter is mostly about knowing which word applies to
each deal you sign — *before* you sign it.

## The rights on your book, one shelf at a time

A book's rights are like a rack of keys. Each one opens a different market:

| Right | What it opens | Typical holder for an indie author |
|---|---|---|
| Print (paperback/hardcover) | Amazon, bookstores, libraries | You (KDP takes non-exclusive print) |
| Digital (ebook) | Kindle, Kobo, Apple, Nook, library apps | You (KDP ebook is non-exclusive) |
| Audio | Audible, Spotify, Apple, library audio apps | Depends on your audio deal |
| Translation | Foreign-language editions | You, until you sell it |
| Film/TV, serial, merch | Hollywood, game studio, comics | You (someone has to actively buy this) |
| Territory | Which countries a deal covers | You, unless a deal says "world" |

The default indie position on KDP: **you keep everything.** KDP's terms for
digital and print are non-exclusive — Amazon is one store among many, day or
year one. That is the baseline this whole guide builds on.

Deals only change that when you **sign a grant of something**. So the habit
that protects your rights is embarrassingly simple:

> **Read for the words "exclusive," "non-exclusive," the name of a format
> (print/audio/ebook), a length of time, and a list of countries.** If any of
> those words sneak past you, stop and read again.

## Exclusivity: the word that matters most

- **Non-exclusive**: you may sell the same edition anywhere else, and you can
  end the deal tomorrow. Amazon KDP (ebook and print) is this.
- **Exclusive**: for the length of the term, that format cannot be sold
  through anyone else — even for a higher price elsewhere. Exclusivity is
  usually *purchased with a better royalty*, not taken for free. If someone
  asks for exclusive rights and offers nothing extra in return, that is the
  deal to be suspicious of.

Exclusivity is not automatically bad. The classic fair trade is ACX: Audible
pays you 40% royalties instead of 25% *because* your audiobook is exclusive
for 7 years. That is a real discount you are choosing to accept — with eyes
open (see [Audiobook routes](04-audiobook-routes.md)).

## Term and reversion

"Term" means how long the deal lasts. Three patterns cover almost everything:

1. **At-will** (KDP): you can leave anytime; your book stays live until you.
2. **Fixed term** (a "7-year audio exclusive," a magazine's 6-month first
   rights): the deal ends in years or months. Mark the date on your calendar.
3. **Term tied to sales** (most traditional publishing contracts): rights
   stay gone until the book stops selling "X copies a year." This is the
   reason to read a publishing contract slowly — a book chugging along at
   400 copies a year may revert decades later or never.

When a term ends, you want a clean **reversion**: a short written notice that
specific rights came back. Keep every agreement and its end date in one
folder — that is literally what the
[worksheet: rights map](worksheets/worksheet-rights-map.md) is for.

## The four situations where indies actually get stuck

| Situation | What to check first |
|---|---|
| You want to run a KDP exclusive promo (e.g., Kindle Unlimited) | Amazon's exclusivity applies to the **digital** edition only, and only while enrolled |
| An "anthology" or magazine wants your story | Usually a **license**, sometimes exclusive for a term — reversion date or walk |
| A small press asks for everything-but-the-printing (world rights, all formats) | You likely lose audio; audio is your most expensive right to replace |
| You co-wrote a chapter of a software/life-admin guide with an LLC | Default: co-owners each hold a share; put the split in writing on paper one day one |

## A plain-English reading checklist

Keep it taped next to your desk (or take it to the
[channel worksheet](worksheets/worksheet-channel-decision.md)):

- [x] "Exclusive or non-exclusive?" — the price of the deal in one word
- [x] "Which format?" — print, digital, audio, subrights, or all of it
- [x] "Which countries?" — "world" against "US/UK/Canada only" are different books' worth of rights
- [x] "How long?" — a date you can put in a calendar is a good sign
- [x] "What do I get specifically for the exclusivity?" — higher royalty, advance, promotion
- [x] "How do I get out?" — notice requirement, reversion letter, who I email

> All of this guide is educational, not legal advice. A self-published ebook
> on KDP needs no contract at all. The first time money with a *third party*
> is on the table — especially exclusive rights to audio — a one-hour review
> by a publishing-savvy attorney is the least expensive insurance in this
> entire guide.
