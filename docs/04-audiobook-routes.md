# Audiobook routes: ACX and the wider world

Audiobooks are the format with the steepest production cost — and the widest
spread of ways to pay for it. There are four honest doors. Two cost almost
nothing, one leases your rights for seven years, and one you can literally
record yourself.

## Door 1: ACX royalty share — no money down

**How it works:** [ACX](https://www.acx.com) (Amazon-owned) lets you publish
your audiobook with a narrator at **no upfront cost**. The narrator and you
split royalties 50/50 for the length of the deal in exchange for them
recording it for free.

What you keep and give:

| | ACX exclusive | ACX non-exclusive |
|---|---|---|
| Your royalty — Audible/Amazon/Apple | **40%** of list | **25%** |
| Where else can it be sold? | Nowhere else, not even your own site | Findaway/D2D, Spotify, Google, libraries — often at higher effective rates |
| Length of term | 7 years | 7 years |
| Narrator deal | RoyaltyShare (50/50 with you) or Pay-Per-Finished-Hour | Same |

**The 7-year term is the part people skim past.** If the audiobook lasts
seven years — and a competent, well-reviewed one does — you cannot move it to
Findaway, D2D, or a new platform whose rates might beat 40%. Non-exclusive ACX
(25%) plus wide distribution (often ~ 40–60% of a given price after the
platform takes its cut) is the safest split for a beginner who wants both
Audible and everywhere else. These percentages are the industry's usual
rounds; verify the current schedule on ACX before deciding.

**Your upfront cost: $0.** That is the beauty of royalty share.

## Door 2: Pay-per-finished-hour (PFH)

You hire a narrator as a contractor. Typical ranges (round numbers; the
blockbuster narrators go higher):

- New-to-intermediate narrator: **$150–250 per finished hour (PFH)**
- Established narrator: **$250–400 PFH**
- A 60,000-word book ≈ **5–5.5 finished hours** (the common rule is roughly
  9,000 words per finished hour)

So a 60k-word book at $200 PFH, on intermediate narrator: **$1,000–1,300
upfront**. All future royalties — 40% exclusive, or 25% ACX non-exclusive,
or whatever wide platforms offer — are **100% yours**; the narrator is paid
once and done.

*Run the numbers before booking anybody*: at $19.99 list with a 40%
exclusive royalty and no ongoing costs, roughly **$8 per sale**, break-even
on ~ $1,200 of upfront audio costs happens around **150 sold units**. The
[worksheet: cost compare](worksheets/worksheet-cost-compare.md) gives you a
fill-in table for this.

## Door 3: Self-recording (the "free" route that is really $80–250)

**Total doable on a rainy Saturday-into-next-Saturday plan.** You need:

- a condenser microphone in a *small reflective space* (think: bed under a
  blanket with a duvet taped around it, closet, clothes rack) — **$0**
- a quiet, foam-free pair of headphones you already own — **$0**
- a copy of [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org) or
  [Reaper](https://www.reaper.fm) free for eval — **$0–60**
- mastering to audiobook volume spec (ACX has a free **"Check AC"** plugin,
  and Findaway/D2D are less strict) — **$0**
- a copy of your manuscript cut into 200-word "chunks" on a cheap teleprompter
  app — **$0**

The actual work that eats time is not the reading. It is the finishing:
punch-in retakes, noise reduction passes, chapter spacing, and proofing —
**roughly 4–6 production hours per finished hour of audio** for a careful
first-timer. If your book is 60k words / 5.5 finished hours, that's roughly
**25–35 focused hours** spread across two weeks of spare time.

It works when: your voice is a *feature* of the product (healthcare explainer
tone, a friendly AI-guide voice), the niche is one where "the author reading"
brings authenticity, and you can trade evenings for a $1,000 production bill.

## Door 4: AI narration — the genuinely cheap experiment

Two real options as of this guide (rates change; verify current):

1. **Apple Books digital narration** — you upload the ebook, pick a voice,
   Apple generates the narration. **Free to you**; Apple then sells the
   audiobook and you collect the standard Apple ebook royalty share. No
   ACX. No DRM. Nothing you record.
2. **Google Play Books auto-narration** — same concept; you upload the
   ebook, generate a machine narration, list it on Google Play. You collect
   the standard Google Play ebook share.

**Important disclosure rule:** AI-narrated audiobooks must be labeled as AI
narration wherever they are sold — this is an ACX/industry norm, and each
platform states its own policy. Audible rejects machine narration altogether;
Spotify, Google, and Apple accept listed AI-narrated titles today. That shelf
difference is the *real* reason people still take Door 1 for the flagship
title.

Round number for planning: **an AI audiobook costs $0 and earns none of the
royalty share** that a human narrator would — its only cost is the
"generated narration" label readers see. Plenty of indie authors run *both*:
a human-narrated audiobook for the flagship title, and a machine-narrated
copy of the guides that would otherwise never get a narrator.

## What each route spends

| Route | Upfront cash | Your royalty rate (round) | Time to ready | You keep full control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ACX royalty share (exclusive) | $0 | 40% Audible etc. | 4–10 weeks | No — 7-year exclusive |
| ACX non-exclusive | $0 | 25% + wide resale | 4–10 weeks | No — 7-year non-exclusive |
| PFH narrator | $1,000–2,500 | 25–100% wide, your choice | 6–10 weeks | Yes, from day one |
| Self-record | ~ $0–250 (gear, tutorials) | 25–100% wide, your choice | 3–6 weeks of evenings | Completely |
| AI narration | $0 | Standard sale share on Apple/Google/Spotify | Days | Completely |

## The one-paragraph recommendation

Most beginner indies with one good book should default to **ACX royalty
share, non-exclusive** — no cash invested, zero editing risk, still legal to
list it on Findaway/D2D/Spotify. Take **exclusive 40%** only if you are sure
Audible will be the bulk of your audio sales for seven years. When your
audiobook has proven ROI, book a **PFH narrator for book two** with 60%
royalty all yours. Reserve self-recording for books where *your* voice is
the point. Keep AI narration as the **fourth** shelf-stock: cheap, labeled,
and useful for a title that would otherwise never get a narrator.
