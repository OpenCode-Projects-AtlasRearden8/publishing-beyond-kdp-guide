Direct sales: the channel where you keep the most

Amazon takes 30% + print costs. Gmail, a landing page, and a checkout widget take almost nothing. Direct sales are the highest-margin channel in publishing — and the only one where the marketing problem is 100% yours. This chapter gives you the fee math and the do-it-in-an-afternoon setup.

The margin math in one table

Assume a $9.99 ebook. Round numbers, including payment-processor fees:

Channel Platform cut of each sale Your money on $9.99
Amazon Kindle (70% tier, small delivery fee) 30% ~ $6.90
Gumroad ~10% + payment fees ~ $8.50
Payhip (free plan) 5% + payment fees ~ $9.00
Payhip (paid plan) 0% + payment fees ~ $9.30
Shopify + digital delivery app ~ 2.9% + 30c + about $29/month fixed ~ $9.30 minus rent
Newsletter spot / in-person payment fees only, ~ 3% + 30c ~ $9.40

And a $4.99 ebook — where "5% of the price" stops sounding small:

Channel Your money on $4.99
Amazon Kindle ~ $3.49
Gumroad ~ $4.20
Payhip (free plan, 5%) ~ $4.60
In-person (Square/Stripe reader) ~ $4.50

Round-number takeaway: direct sales roughly double your per-cent copy on the small-ticket ebooks, and on bundles ("The Massive AI Manual" plus a worksheet pack) they do far better than double, because you set the bundle price.

The honest cost: you are also the Ads department

Direct sales move units for exactly one reason: you already have an audience that will follow a link. Every dollar of margin only counts after someone lands on your page — that someone has to come from your newsletter, Amazon "about the author" pages (allowed to mention your store), speaking slot, video channel, or in-person chats.

So the plan is not "open a Payhip and get rich." It is:

  1. Ship the ebook wide (Amazon + D2D) and use kdp-profit-planner to price it.
  2. Collect emails inside the book: "Download the bonus chapter at [your site]." Even 50 readers over a year of selling is the seed of an audience.
  3. When roughly 300–1,000 emails exist, launch direct sales: the launch email converts at much better rates than any Amazon listing you will ever write.
  4. Keep Amazon. It is the discovery engine. Direct is the margin engine.

Print direct (the harder, juicier trick)

Two ways to sell paperback directly:

  1. You buy print from IngramSpark or Lulu at cost, and sell at events (convention tables, nursing conferences, FFXIV guild meetups that decided you probably are an author anyway). A box of 20 copies, table fees, a Square reader, and one weekend is all it takes. Keep every receipt; they're deductible.
  2. Print-on-demand direct (Lulu's direct-from-store links, or Gumroad's Lulu integration): customer orders; printer ships; you keep cover price minus print minus fee. Slower delivery, no inventory, real margin — and the "also buy from your local library" sentence belongs on this page, since not everyone wants to wait.
A consignment deal with a local indie bookstore ("I leave 5 books, the store takes 30%, unsold copies come back after 30 days") is ordinary business. Put it on a one-page piece of paper, both people sign it, no contract attorney needed at this scale.

Setup-in-an-afternoon, the lazy-good version

  1. Make one page at Payhip (or Ko-fi) holding: cover image, 150-word pitch, buy button, "included files: PDF + EPUB."
  2. Make one page on [your site] (you already have dylanasdale.github.io) — plain HTML is fine — linking to it.
  3. Set the ebook at $9.99 and add the EPUB file (built from your KDP interior, or exported from a DOCX by D2D's converter).
  4. Screenshot the QR code from your Checkout page on your card/table/QR reader. It goes on the bookmarks table and the back of your business card.
  5. Send the launch email to your existing list; put the link on your KDP author page where terms allow.

That's it. You now sell at 85–95% margins. Write the newsletter. The books aren't a hobby anymore.
