Putting it together: the launch sequence

Everything before this was one door at a time. Here is the whole build order, with a budget table and the decision worksheets that keep you honest. The assumed book: a $9.99 ebook, $16.99 paperback, ~60,000 words, produced with the companion projects (interior templates, profit planner).

The order that avoids re-work

Week Step Chapter Cash out
1 Finish interior, export EPUB + PDF kdp-interior-templates ~$0
1 Royalty math, pick prices kdp-profit-planner ~$0
2 KDP paperback + ebook live on Amazon (your existing setup) ~$0
2 Buy Bowker ISBNs (10-pack) — apply to all formats ch. 2 ~$295
3–4 KDP expanded distribution checkbox ON ch. 3 ~$0
3–4 IngramSpark paperback (40% discount, decide on returns) ch. 3 ~$49–69
5 Direct store (Payhip) + QR code + landing page ch. 6 ~$0
5 Newsletter bonus chapter wired into the book ch. 6 ~$0–20
6–8 ACX: invite narrators, non-exclusive first pass ch. 4 ~$0
8+ D2D wide + library distribution ticked ch. 3, ch. 5 ~$0
8+ Indie Author Project submission ch. 5 ~$0
Ongoing Log receipts, quarterly estimate, 30¢ rule ch. 7 —

Total first-title spend if you do everything: about $350–410 — ISBNs being the biggest line by far. The money-light version (skip Bowker, skip IngramSpark) is $0 and still publishes everywhere that matters most.

The three branching decisions, honestly made

1. Do I buy ISBNs? (only real cash commitment)

Run the channel worksheet. If the answer to "will this book touch IngramSpark, bookstores, or libraries within a year?" is yes, or maybe — buy the 10-pack. If the book is a platform-exclusive experiment — skip it. You can always re-edition later; you just reset review momentum, which the worksheet costs out.

2. Which audio door? (the rights-heavy one)

Run the rights map worksheet before ACX, because ACX's 7-year term is the only decision in this guide where "change my mind later" costs a lawyer. The beginner default (non-exclusive royalty share) survives being wrong.

3. Direct sales now or later? (the audience-heavy one)

Direct sales convert only an audience you built. Under ~300 backers of any kind, build the Payhip page anyway (it costs an afternoon) and treat it as your receipt book: every in-person copy, every newsletter launch, every bundle lives there. The channel's real activation is whenever your list is big enough — which is not a money decision at all.

Two worked mini-scenarios

Scenario A — "Amazon-first, zero-risk": KDP paperback + ebook, expanded distribution on, free ISBN, no audio. Cost: $0. Year-one income at 30 copies/month (a realistic niche title): roughly $2,100 royalties. Taxes on ~ $2,100 profit: roughly $530. That's the baseline every other scenario has to beat.

Scenario B — "wide launch": same, plus Bowker 10-pack, IngramSpark at 40% discount, Payhip, ACX non-exclusive with a royalty-share narrator, D2D wide + libraries. Cost: about $350. Year-one income: Amazon roughly the same $2,100, plus ~ $260 wide/library/direct margin (bookstores buy small without an audience; direct is list-dependent), plus audio (bit-early; break-even not reached in year one). Taxes on ~ $1,700 profit after deducting the $350: roughly $430.

Round conclusions: Scenario B beats A in year two or three (if the narrator relationship holds and libraries renew) and is close enough at 50/50 in year one that the deciding factor is whether bookstores or libraries fit your actual book. The worksheets exist precisely because the right answer depends on your book, not on a blog's enthusiasm.

The one-page checklist to run before any "go wide" button

  • Price identical across ISBN-holders (KDP list = IngramSpark list)
  • Copyright page shows the right ISBN for that edition
  • Returns decision made and recorded (IngramSpark is returnable or not)
  • Wholesale discount chosen (40% vs 55%) and it agrees with the math
  • BISAC subjects picked deliberately (2–3, not 12)
  • Cover hardcover vs paperback files are separate and correct
  • Newsletter hook inside the book points at a page that works
  • Every expense this month lands in the receipt folder
  • Companion kdp-profit-planner says the price still breaks even after all of today's decisions

Where this guide ends
